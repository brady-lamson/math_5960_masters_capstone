### New metric - Continuous Ranked Probability Score (CRPS)

I've been desperately wanting for metrics that actually leverage the distribution of estimates we get from the bayesian models. Checking the mean of the estimate distribution is nice and all but flattens all of that information down. There could be additional differences beyond the mean and sd of the county estimates. That's where CRPS comes in.

In laymens terms, it works as an MAE across the entire distribution. It's heavily used in forecasting problems, especially weather forecasting. I implemented it using the `scoringRules` package without any issue.

At the high level, this shows us that all instances of the FH model improve upon the initial ACS estimates. Every single metric improves. The model-to-model differences are quite slim, I need to quantify proportional improvement relevant to the ACS to really get at what's going on. 

At a glance though, we see that the full model has the best MSE, and the intercept only model has the best MAE. The new estimates do actually capture the true value of more counties than the ACS do, though all capture basically the same amount. The ACS captures 73 counties correctly, whereas the others capture 75. The full model gets one extra at 76. That's still over 20 models that aren't captured, so we'll want to examine if these models at the very least improve upon those estimates well. 

The ICAR models have the smallest interval width and sd which is a nice narrative point I can make. The CRPS is lowest on the full model, the other models are all nearly identical there. What's nice is we get additional counties captured AND a reduction in interval width. That's honestly great.
